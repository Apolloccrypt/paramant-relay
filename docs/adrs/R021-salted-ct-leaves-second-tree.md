# R021. Salted CT leaves in a second tree, with the first frozen

Date: 2026-09-05

Status: Proposed. Nothing in this document is built.

Deciders: Mick (project owner)

Relates to: R013 (CT-log usage), R017 (.psign envelope format), and the
timestamp and persistence repairs that ship alongside this file.

## Context

A leaf in the transparency log is an unsalted hash of guessable inputs:

```
transfer leaf  = SHA3-256(0x02 || sha256(file) || SHA3-256(sector) || utf8(ts))
parasign leaf  = SHA3-256(0x00 || signer_pk_hash || document_hash || utf8(ts))
```

Nothing in there is secret from someone holding a copy of the document. The
file hash is his, the sector is one of five, and the timestamp is what the log
publishes. Rounding the published time to the hour leaves 3.6e6 x 5 candidates:
**measured at 57 seconds on one core**, 323,000 leaf hashes per second in plain
Node with no optimisation. So anyone with a copy of a document can prove it went
through Paramant and recover the exact millisecond, without an account, a court
order, or asking Paramant anything. For a signature it is worse: the leaf binds
the signer's key hash to the document hash, which is precisely the fact a
signing service exists to protect.

Two routes made that free rather than merely cheap, and both are fixed in the
change this file ships with: `/ct/feed` published the raw millisecond, and the
signed tree head carried `Date.now()` from the same append. Both now round to
the hour. That closes the shortcut. It does not close the attack: 57 seconds is
not protection, it is a delay.

The EDPB is explicit that an unsalted hash is personal data and that unsalted
hashes are insufficient (Guidelines 02/2025, v1.1). CNIL's preferred ordering
puts a hiding commitment first, and holds that after the witness value is
destroyed a perfectly hiding commitment is anonymous. The whole legal difference
is one 32-byte field. `dpa.html` currently says the CT log may be retained after
termination because it holds "only hashed identifiers, no payload content"; a
hash that is testable against a candidate in 57 seconds does not carry that
sentence.

## Decision

The repair is one field:

```
leaf = SHA3-256(0x03 || salt[32] || canonicalJSON(event))
```

with the salt generated by a CSPRNG **in the customer's browser**, carried in
his receipt, and never sent to the relay. Without the salt a leaf is a 32-byte
random number. With it, the holder proves his own row in one hash. Paramant
cannot confirm anything about a document it is shown, and that is a property
rather than a promise.

It cannot be applied retroactively: the 4,780 existing leaves stay guessable
whatever we do next. So the existing tree is **frozen, not rewritten**: a final
signed tree head, read-only from then on, served for as long as any receipt
issued from it might still be checked. This is RFC 9162 section 4.13, and it is
what Chrome does with a retired CT log: a `final_tree_head` published in a list
Google maintains, not by the log itself. The trust anchor does not live with the
party that can disappear.

## What this costs, honestly

This is why it is not in the pull request that fixes the timestamps. The
single-tree assumption is not localised; it is the shape of the whole surface.

**One namespace, assumed everywhere.** `size`, `root` and `index` appear
unqualified in `/v2/ct/log`, `/ct/feed`, `/v2/ct/proof`, `/v2/sth`, in
`frontend/js/ct-log.page.js` (one total, one root), in
`scripts/heartbeat/parasign.mjs` (which correlates a global index between the
listing and the proof), in `relayRegistryFromCTLog()`, and in the `metrics`
gauge. The built-in `/ct` page cross-checks `/ct/feed` against `/v2/sth` for
equality, which two trees break by construction.

**`produceSth` is single-tree.** One `sthLog`, one `STH_FILE`, a hardcoded
`version: 1`, and a head produced on every append. Freezing tree one means
suppressing its heads while tree two keeps producing them, which means splitting
the head store, the file, and the `/v2/sth*` routes.

**The salt cannot reach the server, but two server paths need the leaf.** The
receipt build (`relay.js`, the `/v2/outbound/:hash` handler) and
`POST /v2/verify-receipt` both recompute `blobLeafHash` from the blob hash, the
sector and the ts. With a browser-only salt the server cannot. Either the salt
travels in the receipt (which costs nothing, the receipt is the customer's),
or the `leaf_hash_mismatch` check moves client-only and
`frontend/js/receipt-verify.js` becomes the single place a leaf is bound to a
blob. That is a decision about defence in depth, not a detail.

**`.psign` carries a bare index.** `notary.ct_log_index` is an integer and
`ct_log_url` points at `/v2/ct/log`, both inside the notary-signed canonical
JSON. Two trees make that integer ambiguous. Adding a discriminator invalidates
nothing already issued but has to move in lockstep across `relay/parasign.js`,
`relay/lib/parasign-open-api.js`, `frontend/parasign-verify.js`, R017 and
`relay/test/qes-receipt.test.js`.

**`/v2/sth/consistency` is bounded by the window, not the log.** It reads
`ctWindow.windowLength`, so a frozen tree that has aged past `CT_MAX` cannot
produce a consistency proof to its own final head. Freezing has to happen while
the whole tree is still in the window, or the window has to stop being the
bound.

**The verification page has to hold both eras at once.** An old receipt has no
salt and must keep verifying against the frozen tree and its final head; a new
one verifies against the salted tree. `frontend/js/receipt-verify.js` needs a
leaf recipe selected by the receipt rather than hardcoded, and
`relay-trust-anchors.js` needs the frozen tree's final head as an anchor that
outlives the relay serving it. Getting this wrong breaks every receipt ever
issued, which is the failure mode already seen once this week when the
verification page knew one relay key out of five.

**The nginx allow-list and the two internal public-path lists** name routes
literally, so every new route has to be added in three places or it 404s in
production and passes in test.

## Order of work, if it is taken up

1. Split `produceSth` and the head store per tree; give a tree an id and a
   version. This is the piece everything else waits on.
2. Freeze tree one: a final head, published in a small signed, hash-pinned list
   that does not live with the relay, and a read-only mode for the window.
   Do it before the tree ages past `CT_MAX`.
3. `0x03` salted leaves in tree two, salt generated in the browser and carried
   in the receipt, `canonicalJSON` over the event.
4. Two-era verification in `receipt-verify.js`, with a test that mints a receipt
   from each era and passes both. Written before step 3 ships, not after.
5. `.psign` gains a tree discriminator; R017 updated in the same commit.

## Consequences

Until this is built, the log's protection against a confirmation attack is 57
seconds of CPU and nothing else, and the retention clause in the DPA rests on a
reading of "hashed identifier" the EDPB has written against. That is the honest
statement of where the product stands, and it belongs in the DPA text rather
than in a design document nobody outside reads.

The scale of the work above is also the argument for taking it seriously rather
than soon. Every one of those coupling points is a place where a half-finished
second tree would break a receipt that works today, and a broken receipt is
worse than a guessable one: the guessable one at least still proves what it
claims to prove.
