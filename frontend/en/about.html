About Paramant

With Paramant you send documents and get them signed, as a practice or an office.

Mick Beer
Privacy and security researcher, founder of Paramantis Solutions B.V.

I research how personal data leaks from the apps and clouds people trust. My articles and tools are on mickbeer.com.

Paramant is what I would want a small firm to have: a way to get a document signed and send a file, with client files that stay in Europe and proof you can check without us. Think of a patient file that has to reach a colleague, or a court document that has to come back signed. What it costs is on the pricing page.

How it works

You pick a file and your browser encrypts it before it leaves your computer. Our server keeps only that encrypted copy, in memory and never on a disk. After the last pickup it is deleted.

Getting a document signed happens in the browser too. The key you sign with is made on your own device and never reaches our servers. Every signature is entered into a public, append-only log. A signed document verifies without contacting us. You can check one on the verify page.

Legally, a signature made with Paramant is a Simple Electronic Signature (SES). It is not a qualified signature (QES) under eIDAS.

What it costs

The Community plan costs €0, forever. It gives you 2 signatures a month and 50 transfers a month, with one recipient per send. No card required, and no trial that runs out. The Community plan is my way of giving something back to society; the business plans pay for it.

Firm is 29 euro a month excl. VAT. It gives you 100 signatures a month and 500 transfers a month, up to 30 recipients per send. Every plan gets the same encryption and the same public log. Need more? Email me: privacy@paramant.app

Under the hood

The servers are in Germany, under EU law. When you hand a file over directly, Paramant uses a hybrid post-quantum key exchange: a new method built to withstand the quantum computers of the future, together with a proven classical one. Signatures are fully post-quantum. The source code is public on GitHub, and the details are on the security page.

The names of the algorithms

Signing uses ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204), generated in your browser. The private key never reaches our servers. A direct hand-over uses ML-KEM-768 together with ECDH P-256, then AES-256-GCM. A plain link uses AES-256-GCM with a key that sits only in the link itself.
